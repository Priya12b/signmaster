Basic ASL Sentence Structure

Learn how American Sign Language grammar and word order fundamentally differ from English syntax.

1. The Topic-Comment Structure (Primary ASL Syntax)

Unlike spoken English (which is strictly Subject-Verb-Object), ASL syntax primarily employs a Topic-Comment framework. The signer establishes the topic first (with raised eyebrows), followed by the comment or action regarding it.

Examples:
  • STORE I GO → "I am going to the store."
  • DOG SLEEP → "The dog is sleeping."
  • PIZZA I LIKE → "I like pizza."

2. Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) in ASL

ASL also permits SVO structures for short, direct statements or active imperative commands:

  • I LOVE YOU
  • YOU CLOSE DOOR
  • YESTERDAY I GO PARK

3. Time Markers & Context

ASL does not conjugate verbs for tense (there is no past, present, or future tense ending like -ed or -ing). Instead, signers place a Time Marker at the very beginning of the sentence:

Formula: TIME + TOPIC + COMMENT
Example: YESTERDAY HOMEWORK I FINISH = "I finished my homework yesterday."

4. Non-Manual Markers (NMM)

Facial expressions, eyebrow postures (raised for Yes/No questions, furrowed for WH-questions), and head tilts constitute 50% of ASL grammar. Signing without facial grammar is like speaking in a robotic monotone without pitch modulation.
