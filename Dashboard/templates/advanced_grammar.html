ASL Grammar & Fluency Guide

Master topicalization, non-manual markers, 3D signing space, and spatial verb movements.

1. Introduction to ASL Linguistic Independence

A common misconception is that ASL is simply English rendered through gestures. In reality, American Sign Language is an autonomous, complete natural language with distinct syntax, phonology (parameters), and morphology.

2. Topicalization & Word Order

ASL commonly places the topic (the object of interest) at the start of the clause, followed by an assertion about that topic.

3. Non-Manual Markers (NMM)

Non-manual markers include eyebrow motion, mouth morphemes (e.g., 'cha', 'mm', 'th'), and shoulder shifts. They determine whether an utterance is a question, negation, conditional, or assertion.

4. 3D Signing Space & Referent Loci

Signers set up referents (people, places, concepts) in the physical space in front of them (loci) and direct verbs toward those loci without repeating names.

5. Directional & Spatial Verbs

Directional verbs (such as GIVE, HELP, SHOW, ASK) alter their starting and ending points in space to indicate who did what to whom.

6. Classifiers & Depicting Signs

Classifiers represent shape, size, location, and motion (e.g. CL:3 for land vehicles, CL:1 for people walking, CL:B for flat surfaces).
